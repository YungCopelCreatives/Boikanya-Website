Pre- and Post-Natal Movement Support: Evidence-Based Care for Mothers

By Boikanya Biokinetics Team • Updated Jan 2026
5 min read

Pregnancy and postpartum are among the most physiologically transformative experiences in a woman's life. Hormonal shifts (notably increased relaxin), postural adaptations, shifts in the centre of gravity, and abdominal wall stretching all require specialized physical activity guidance.

Why Exercise During Pregnancy Matters

According to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) and the Biokinetics Association of South Africa (BASA), regular, structured exercise during uncomplicated pregnancies confers major health benefits:

  • Gestational Diabetes & Preeclampsia Prevention: Improves insulin sensitivity and regulates vascular blood pressure.
  • Pelvic Girdle & Lumbar Pain Relief: Stabilizes the sacroiliac (SI) joints and alleviates low back strain caused by anterior pelvic tilt.
  • Labour Endurance & Delivery Preparation: Strengthens the deep abdominal wall, gluteal complex, and diaphragm.
  • Mental Well-being: Reduces prenatal anxiety and optimizes sleep patterns.

Clinical Safety First

All prenatal programmes are adapted to your trimester, obstetrician clearance, and individual symptoms, avoiding prone positions after the first trimester and preventing excessive intra-abdominal pressure.

Postpartum Rehabilitation: The 4th Trimester

Recovery does not conclude with birth. The postpartum phase requires systematic rehabilitation to restore tissue integrity before returning to high-impact exercise (such as running or heavy lifting):

1. Diastasis Recti Abdominis (DRA) Assessment

We measure inter-recti distance (IRD) and, more importantly, evaluate the functional tension and responsiveness of the linea alba and transverse abdominis under load.

2. Pelvic Floor & Deep Core Synchronization

Rather than isolated crunches (which can worsen abdominal separation), we train diaphragmatic breathing synchronized with co-contraction of the pelvic floor and deep transverse abdominis muscles.

3. Functional Postural Restoration

Caring for a newborn involves repeated asymmetrical loading—nursing, lifting, carrying car seats, and pushing prams. We strengthen the posterior chain (rhomboids, lats, glutes) to counteract upper crossed syndrome and thoracic tightness.

When to Begin?

Gentle breathing and pelvic stabilization can begin within days of uncomplicated birth, while structured progressive exercise typically starts following your 6-week obstetrician or midwife check-up.
